Red Drum vs. black drum: how to tell them apart

Two drum, two silhouettes. Barbels, eyespots and tail shape separate them instantly.

Fast comparison

FeatureRed DrumBlack Drum
Chin barbelsNoneSeveral short barbels under the chin
Tail spotsOne or more dark eyespots near the tail baseNo eyespots; juveniles show dark vertical bars instead
Tail shapeTruncated, slightly curved edgeBroad and rounded
MouthDown-turned, feed-forward snoutPavement teeth; crushes shell on structure

Photograph these features

  1. Underside of the chin for barbels
  2. Tail-base close-up for eyespots
  3. Tail spread to show the edge shape
  4. Juveniles: capture any vertical bars

When it is not clear-cut

Juvenile black drum bars fade with age and bull-reds lose spots entirely some years — the chin barbels remain the constant. Big "bull" reds and black drum overlap in size and water.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which one "drums" louder?
Black drum are the louder croakers; you can hear them through a boat hull on calm days.
Which is the table favorite?
Red drum (as "redfish") dominate Gulf menus; small black drum ("puppy drum" size blacks) also rate well — large blacks get coarse.
Do they hybridize?
No confirmed hybrids; the two are distinguished easily by barbels and eyespots.
