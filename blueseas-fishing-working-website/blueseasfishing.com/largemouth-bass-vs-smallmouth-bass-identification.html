Largemouth Bass vs. smallmouth bass: how to tell them apart

The classic bass confusion. Jaw length, dorsal-fin gap and color pattern separate them in seconds once you know where to look.

Fast comparison

FeatureLargemouth BassSmallmouth Bass
Mouth / jawUpper jaw extends well past the rear of the eyeUpper jaw stops at or before the rear of the eye
Dorsal finDeep notch between spined and soft sectionsShallow notch; the two sections nearly merge
Color patternGreen sides with a dark lateral blotch chainBrown-bronze sides with dark vertical bars
Preferred waterWarm, weedy lakes, ponds and slow river banksCooler, clearer water with rock, gravel and current

Photograph these features

  1. Straight side view with the mouth closed to show jaw length
  2. Dorsal fin spread so the notch depth is visible
  3. Full flank to capture bars versus lateral blotch
  4. Habitat shot — weeds or rock support the call

When it is not clear-cut

Hybrids of the two occur in some waters and share traits of both. Where the jaw and dorsal clues conflict, treat the call as uncertain and rely on the location and habitat.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which grows larger?
Largemouth bass typically grow larger; trophy smallmouth are heavy for their length but usually run smaller overall.
Do they live in the same lakes?
Often in the same reservoir, but largemouth hold in vegetation and wood while smallmouth favor rocky points and current.
Which fights harder?
Smallmouth are famous for acrobatic, sustained runs relative to size; largemouth fight deeper with head shakes.
