Salmon vs. trout: how to tell them apart

There is no single feature that separates every salmon from every trout. Some are close relatives in the same genus, and appearance changes with age, habitat and spawning condition. Use a combination of mouth, tail, spotting, fins and catch location.

Start with these five observations

  1. Mouth and gums: photograph the inside of the mouth and the gum line.
  2. Tail spotting: note whether spots cover the upper lobe, both lobes or neither.
  3. Side pattern: look for a pink stripe, large spots, vertical juvenile marks or spawning colors.
  4. Anal fin: count rays only if the photograph is sharp and the fin is fully visible.
  5. Location and water: freshwater, estuary and ocean context can narrow the possibilities.

Why photographs are misidentified

A bright reflection can erase a side stripe, a closed mouth hides the gums, and a hand placed over the tail removes one of the best clues. Spawning colors can also make the same species look dramatically different. Ask for several angles instead of relying on one attractive side photograph.

Identification tools can be wrong. When a decision affects harvest, possession, consumption or a protected species, consult the local fisheries authority or an experienced biologist.
