Bluegill vs. green sunfish: how to tell them apart

Color changes with water, season, sex and age, so begin with structure. Mouth size and pectoral-fin shape are usually more dependable than overall color.

Fast comparison

FeatureBluegillGreen sunfish
MouthSmall; jaw normally does not reach beyond the eyeLarger; lower jaw reaches about the middle of the eye
Pectoral finLong and pointedShort and rounded
Ear flapSolid dark flap without a pale borderDark flap commonly edged in white, yellow or orange
Body and finsVertical side bars and a dark spot near the rear of the soft dorsal finBlue-green cheek streaks; yellow or orange fin margins are common

Photograph these features

  1. Take a straight side view with the mouth closed.
  2. Spread the pectoral fin so its pointed or rounded shape is visible.
  3. Capture the ear flap and rear portion of the dorsal fin.
  4. Do not rely on belly color alone.

Hybrids can defeat a visual checklist

Bluegill and green sunfish can hybridize. A hybrid may combine a bluegill-like body with a larger mouth or pale fin margins. When the traits conflict, record the location and several photographs and treat the result as uncertain.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.
