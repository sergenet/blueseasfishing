Chinook Salmon vs. coho salmon: how to tell them apart

The two big Pacific salmon. Tail spots and gum line are the timeless checks.

Advertisement

Fast comparison

FeatureChinook SalmonCoho Salmon
Tail spotsSpots on both tail lobes and the backSpots only on the upper tail lobe
Gum lineBlack at the base of the teethWhite to pale gray gums
SizeLargest Pacific salmon — 15–40 lb common in runsSmaller — 6–12 lb typical
Tail shapeBroad, more squareMore forked

Photograph these features

  1. Tail spread for spot coverage
  2. Lower jaw gum line in good light
  3. Full fish with something for scale
  4. Fin tips — coho can show white gum-adjacent edges

When it is not clear-cut

In rivers, both darken and the colors lie; tail spots and gum line remain readable on spawned fish. Landlocked kings (where stocked) still show the double-lobe spots.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which fights harder?
Chinook dive deep and bulldog; coho are famous acrobats.
Which is "king" salmon?
Chinook — the same fish by another name.
Do they run together?
Their runs often overlap by weeks; chinook typically peak earlier in a system.
