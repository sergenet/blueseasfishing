Yellowfin Tuna vs. bluefin tuna: how to tell them apart

The ocean's two great tunas. Fin length and pectoral color settle it at the rail.

Fast comparison

FeatureYellowfin TunaBluefin Tuna
Second dorsal + anal finsLong, sickle-shaped in adults — often past the tail baseShort and rounded at all sizes
Pectoral finLong, reaching well past the fin base; yellow tintShort, stiff, dark
Body buildSlender torpedoThick, football-shaped trunk
SizeCommonly 30–150 lb50 lb school fish to giants past 500 lb

Photograph these features

  1. Dorsal and anal fins spread for length
  2. Pectoral fin folded against the body
  3. Girth from above
  4. Tail keels — yellowfin show twin small keels

When it is not clear-cut

Bigeye tuna sit between the two: longer fins than bluefin but rounder than yellowfin, with a distinct anal fin. When in doubt among all three, photograph the fins and girth.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the sushi star?
Bluefin fetch the record market prices; yellowfin (ahi) is the everyday sushi favorite.
Do they school together?
Often mix offshore with porpoise schools; size classes usually separate by depth.
Which fights harder?
Bluefin — the deepest runs and longest battles in sportfishing.
