Paralichthys californicus

How to identify a California halibut

Look for a large mouth with conical teeth and a strongly arched lateral line above the pectoral fin. Unlike Pacific halibut, California halibut may have both eyes on either the right or left side.

Identification checklist

  • The eyed side is usually brown to brownish-black and can change pattern to match the bottom.
  • The blind side is generally white, although some mottling can occur.
  • The lateral line forms a conspicuous high arch over the pectoral fin.
  • The mouth is large, with conical teeth; the upper jaw extends beyond the eye.
  • The eyes may occur on either side of the head.

California halibut vs. Pacific halibut

Upper jawExtends beyond the eyeEnds near the front edge of the eye

Habitat

California halibut are estuarine and inner-shelf predators, often encountered in bays and shallow coastal water. They can occur deeper but are frequently caught by anglers in roughly 10 to 90 feet of water.
