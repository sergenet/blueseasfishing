Walleye vs. sauger: a practical identification checklist

Both species have elongated bodies, large reflective eyes and teeth. The first dorsal fin and lower tail lobe provide stronger clues than overall color.

Take the right photographs

  1. Raise the first spiny dorsal fin and photograph it squarely.
  2. Include the entire tail, especially the lower lobe.
  3. Capture both sides because blotches can be uneven.
  4. Photograph the cheek and record the waterbody.

Watch for saugeye

Saugeye are walleye-sauger hybrids and can combine traits from both parents. External appearance is not always enough for a certain identification. If the dorsal fin shows both a rear blotch and rows or streaks of dark markings, treat the fish as potentially hybrid and seek local help.

Local rules may differ by species. If the identification affects a size or bag limit, do not guess. Consult the current fishing regulations or a fisheries biologist.
