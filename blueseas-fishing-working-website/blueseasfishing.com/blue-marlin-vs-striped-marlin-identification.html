Blue Marlin vs. striped marlin: how to tell them apart

Pacific big-game royalty. Dorsal height, stripes and build separate the two.

Advertisement

Fast comparison

FeatureBlue MarlinStriped Marlin
First dorsal finTall but lower than body depth; rounded profileTaller than body depth; pointed, pocketed membranes
StripesLight blue bars that fade quickly after landingVivid lavender-blue vertical bars, persist when lit up
BuildDeep-chested, heavy forward shoulderSleeker, more compressed body
BillLong, round, robustLonger relative to body, more slender

Photograph these features

  1. Dorsal fin raised against body depth
  2. Flank lit up for stripe color
  3. Bill-to-body proportion from the side
  4. Boat-side girth for the build difference

When it is not clear-cut

Black marlin join the party in the Indo-Pacific and lack the vivid bars; their pectoral fins do not fold flat. Compare dorsal and pectoral position in every billfish photo.

Frequently asked questions

Which is bigger?
Blue marlin — granders past 1,000 lb exist; striped marlin rarely pass 300 lb.
Which jumps more?
Striped marlin are famous for repeated tail-walking; blues run deeper.
Where do they overlap?
Pacific offshore canyons and banks — Kona, Cabo, and the great seamounts.
