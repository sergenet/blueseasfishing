Rainbow Trout vs. cutthroat trout: how to tell them apart

One look under the jaw: the cutthroat's red-orange slash is the namesake and the giveaway.

Fast comparison

FeatureRainbow TroutCutthroat Trout
Jaw slashNo slash under the lower jawDistinct red-orange slash under the jaw
Lateral stripePink to rose lateral stripe, commonUsually absent or faint
SpottingDense small black spots, tail heavily spottedCoarser spots, concentrated toward the tail
MaxillaEnds near the eyeOften extends past the eye

Photograph these features

  1. Lift the jaw to show or rule out the slash
  2. Full flank for the stripe and spots
  3. Head profile for maxilla length
  4. Even light — colors shift fast in spawning fish

When it is not clear-cut

Steelhead are the sea-run form of rainbow trout and lose the stripe; the slash check still separates them from sea-run cutthroat. Hybrid "cutbows" blur both — note the location.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cutbow?
A rainbow × cutthroat hybrid, common where stocks overlap; it may show a faint slash and a pink stripe together.
Which is native where?
Cutthroats are the native western mountain fish; rainbows now range worldwide through stocking.
Same flies?
Yes — both take standard trout fare; cutthroats in remote water are famously aggressive.
