Brook Trout vs. brown trout: how to tell them apart

A char versus a true trout: vermiculation and white-edged fins versus red halos and a square tail.

Fast comparison

FeatureBrook TroutBrown Trout
Back patternWorm-like pale vermiculation linesPlain or with dark spots; no worm lines
Flank spotsPale yellow spots; red spots with blue halosRed-black spots, many ringed with pale halos
FinsWhite leading edges with black stripingPlain fins, no white-black edge
TailNearly squareSlightly forked

Photograph these features

  1. Back from above for vermiculation
  2. Pectoral or pelvic fin edge close-up
  3. Flank in even light for halo pattern
  4. Tail spread for the square-versus-forked check

When it is not clear-cut

Brook trout are char (Salvelinus), browns are true salmonids — evolution explains the look. Tiger trout (brook × brown) show dramatic maze vermiculation where stocked.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is a brook trout really a trout?
Technically a char — same family, different genus. Anglers still call it a trout.
Which is more cold-water demanding?
Brook trout need the coldest, cleanest water of the two.
What is a tiger trout?
The striking sterile hybrid of the two, stocked in some states for trophy fishing.
