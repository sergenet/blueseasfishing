Largemouth Bass vs. spotted bass: how to tell them apart

Spotted bass increasingly share largemouth water. One mouth-length check and one tongue check settle most IDs.

Fast comparison

FeatureLargemouth BassSpotted Bass
Mouth / jawUpper jaw extends past the rear of the eyeUpper jaw stops at or just short of the eye
Dorsal finClear notch between the two sectionsSections connected by a low arch, nearly continuous
Flank patternDark lateral blotch chain along the sideRows of small spots below the lateral line
TongueSmooth tongueRough tooth patch at the base of the tongue

Photograph these features

  1. Closed-mouth side view for jaw length
  2. Open mouth angled to reveal the tongue base
  3. Dorsal fin profile showing notch versus arch
  4. Flank shot of the spot rows versus blotch

When it is not clear-cut

The tongue patch is the most reliable single clue but requires a careful look. Hybrids blur features; photograph the jaw, dorsal and flank and compare at home.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which grows larger?
Largemouth grow notably larger in most waters; spotted bass rarely reach trophy largemouth proportions.
Are spotted bass bad for a lake?
They compete with largemouth in some systems, but management views vary by state — check local agency guidance.
How common is the tongue patch check?
Common among bass anglers; run a fingertip gently along the tongue base — spotted bass show a rough patch.
