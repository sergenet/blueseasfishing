King Mackerel vs. spanish mackerel: how to tell them apart

The speed family. Size and the lateral-line "drop" separate kings from spaniards.

Advertisement

Fast comparison

FeatureKing MackerelSpanish Mackerel
SizeCommonly 10–40 lb; giants past 60 lb1–5 lb; rarely past 10 lb
Lateral lineDips sharply below the second dorsal finGently slopes the full length
SpotsUsually spotless; juveniles can show spotsBronze oval spots on the flank
Dorsal finGray front edgeDistinctly black front edge on the first dorsal

Photograph these features

  1. Lateral line trace from gill plate to tail
  2. First dorsal fin edge color
  3. Flank for spots
  4. Girth photo — size gap is the quickest tell

When it is not clear-cut

Small "school" kings muddy the waters: they can carry spots like spanish. The lateral-line dip is the published separator — trace it in the photo.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better eating?
Small spanish are clean and mild; kings are favored for smoking and steaks.
Which is the tournament fish?
King mackerel — the SKA circuit is built around them.
Both bite the same tackle?
Similar trolled spoons and live bait; kings demand wire leaders.
