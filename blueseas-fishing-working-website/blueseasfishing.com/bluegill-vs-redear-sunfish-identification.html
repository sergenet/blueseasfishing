Bluegill vs. redear sunfish: how to tell them apart

The ear flap ends the argument: solid black versus black with a red-orange edge.

Advertisement

Fast comparison

FeatureBluegillRedear Sunfish
Ear flap (gill cover)Solid dark flap, no colored borderDark flap edged in red to orange
MouthSmall mouth; jaw short of the eyeSmall mouth, slightly longer on adults
Body colorDeep blue-green with orange breastOlive-gold, plainer sides, longer body
Feeding habitInsects and larvae throughout the weedsSnails and mussels — the "shellcracker"

Photograph these features

  1. Ear flap macro — the giveaway
  2. Breast color in natural light
  3. Full side profile for body depth
  4. If possible, the bait/habitat (snail shells nearby)

When it is not clear-cut

Hybrid bluegill × redear are stocked in some ponds and show mixed flap color. When in doubt, the flap edge plus body length (redear run longer) settles it.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Why "shellcracker"?
Redear crush snails and mussels with specialized throat teeth — their droppings even glitter with shell.
Which grows bigger?
Redear typically outgrow bluegill in the same water.
Same baits?
Both take worms and crickets; redear fish best slowly on the bottom near shell beds.
