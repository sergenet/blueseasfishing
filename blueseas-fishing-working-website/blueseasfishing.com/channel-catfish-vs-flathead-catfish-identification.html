Channel Catfish vs. flathead catfish: how to tell them apart

A forked tail versus a rounded one, and two totally different feeding styles — the ID mirrors the biology.

Advertisement

Fast comparison

FeatureChannel CatfishFlathead Catfish
TailDeeply forkedSlightly forked to rounded
Head / jawPointed snout, upper jaw even with lowerBroad flat head; lower jaw sticks out past the upper
ColorSlate to olive with scattered dark spotsYellow-olive to chocolate brown, plain or mottled
FeedingOpportunistic scavenger — cut bait, stink baitLive-fish predator — sunfish, shad, live bait rigs

Photograph these features

  1. Tail spread to show fork depth
  2. Head profile from the side showing jaw overlap
  3. Full body color in even light
  4. Mouth view — flatheads look distinctly "bull-dog"

When it is not clear-cut

Flathead color shifts with water clarity; the protruding lower jaw and tail shape hold up in any water. Young flatheads can look plain like channels — check the jaw.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which is more active at night?
Both feed at night, but channel catfish roam in schools; flatheads hunt from cover, often live-bait only.
Which grows bigger?
Flatheads reach larger typical sizes; both produce century-class river giants in southern reservoirs.
Do channels eat live fish too?
Yes, especially at dusk, but their scavenger streak makes them far easier to bait.
