Northern Pike vs. muskellunge: how to tell them apart

The esox cousins: cheek scales, spot-versus-bar patterns and tail shape tell them apart.

Fast comparison

FeatureNorthern PikeMuskellunge
Cheek scalesFully scaled cheek and gill coverLower half of the cheek bare (scaleless patch)
PatternLight bean-shaped spots on dark bodyVertical dark bars on a lighter body
TailMore forkedRounded
SizeCommonly 24–40 inCommonly 36–50 in where established

Photograph these features

  1. Cheek close-up — scales or bare skin
  2. Full flank for spot-versus-bar pattern
  3. Tail spread for edge shape
  4. Head profile — muskie jaws look longer and duck-billed

When it is not clear-cut

Tiger muskellunge (pike × muskie) show bars plus spots and the muskie cheek patch; they are stocked in several states. Juvenile muskie can be spot-heavy — check the cheek.

Identification tools can be wrong. Confirm uncertain fish with the local fisheries authority before relying on the result for harvest or possession rules.

Frequently asked questions

Which is harder to catch?
Muskellunge — the "fish of ten thousand casts." Pike are far more willing.
Do they share water?
Yes across the northern tier; pike school shallower, muskie hold deeper structure.
Which bites more in summer?
Pike keep biting through summer heat; muskie often shift to dawn, dusk and night.
